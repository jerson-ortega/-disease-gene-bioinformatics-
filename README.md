## 7. Reflection
1. **What did UCSC show you about your gene that was not obvious from simply reading about the gene's function?**
   UCSC revealed the sheer physical scale of *MYH7* on chromosome 14, highlighting how its 40 exons are fragmented across thousands of base pairs separated by long introns, which is impossible to appreciate from text descriptions alone[span_30](start_span)[span_30](end_span).

2. **Why is knowing the exact genomic location of a disease-associated variant useful?**
   Pinpointing the exact coordinate allows researchers and clinicians to design precise PCR primers, target specific clinical sequencing panels, and map variants directly relative to functional domains and regulatory splicing junctions[span_31](start_span)[span_31](end_span).

3. **What is one limitation of predicting a variant's effect only from its genomic location?**
   Knowing a variant is in a coding exon does not automatically quantify the severity of the functional change, as some amino acid substitutions may be well-tolerated while others cause catastrophic misfolding[span_32](start_span)[span_32](end_span).

4. **What was the most interesting feature you observed about your assigned gene?**
   The tight alignment between high evolutionary conservation peaks in the comparative tracks and the actual exon boundaries of the myosin motor head domain vividly illustrates how structure dictates function across species[span_33](start_span)[span_33](end_span).
