## 3. Exons, Introns, and Transcripts
* **Number of Exons:** 40 total exons (38 coding exons) based on transcript NM_000257.4.
* **Multiple Transcripts/Isoforms:** Yes, multiple alternative transcript isoforms are visible in the comprehensive gene annotation tracks.
* **Definition of Exon vs. Intron:** Exons are segments of the gene retained in the mature messenger RNA (mRNA) following splicing (some code for amino acids, others form UTRs), whereas introns are intervening non-coding sequences spliced out during transcription processing.
* **Length Comparison:** Introns in the *MYH7* gene are generally much longer than the individual exon blocks, which is characteristic of large human protein-coding genes.

## 6. Locating the Variant in UCSC
* **Location Relative to Gene:** Located within the structural body of the *MYH7* gene on chromosome 14.
* **Genomic Feature Type:** Located inside an exon (specifically coding exon 11).
* **Coding vs. Non-Coding:** Falls directly in a coding region, causing an amino acid substitution in the motor head domain of the protein.
* **Potential Effect:** The substitution of Arginine with Tryptophan (p.Arg403Trp) alters protein folding, charge distribution, and ATP-dependent actin-binding properties, driving hypercontractility and clinical myocardial hypertrophy.
* **Additional Evidence Needed:** Family co-segregation analysis, functional assay validation in cardiomyocytes, and clinical exclusion of secondary genetic modifiers.

## 7. Reflection
1. **What did UCSC show you about your gene that was not obvious from simply reading about the gene's function?**
   UCSC revealed the physical layout and structural size of *MYH7* on chromosome 14, illustrating how its 40 exons span tens of thousands of base pairs separated by extensive intronic regions.

2. **Why is knowing the exact genomic location of a disease-associated variant useful?**
   Pinpointing exact coordinates enables researchers and clinicians to design targeted PCR assays, build accurate custom sequencing panels, and map variants precisely relative to critical functional domains.

3. **What is one limitation of predicting a variant's effect only from its genomic location?**
   Knowing a variant resides within a coding exon does not automatically quantify the severity of the functional defect, as different amino acid substitutions at the same position can have varying phenotypic consequences.

4. **What was the most interesting feature you observed about your assigned gene?**
   The close correspondence between high evolutionary conservation peaks and the exact boundaries of exons in the motor head domain highlights how structural constraints dictate functional importance across species.
